## The signage pre-empt (read this before either declaration)

ARCHETYPE_DECISIONS says signage returns, fabrication-honest, for the
stores family. The Stage 2 gate constraint says no signage in GATE
frames. **These do not conflict**: the gate constraint governs what
appears in gate evidence framings; the archetype vocabulary governs
what the building IS. A marketplace may carry printed-card signage as
its own honest fabric and still never show it in a gate frame. Stated
here once so no future reader of the two documents manufactures a
contradiction.

## 1. STRATEGY MARKETPLACE

**Soul** (inherits the owner's stores-archetype soul, scaled up):
- A MARKET HALL, not a shop: standalone one-to-two-storey hall massing
  with its own roof story — long ridge, clerestory band — plus the
  street-life layer at full strength: stalls, awnings, spill-out goods,
  trade visible outside. The busiest place on the board by design.
- **Landmark legibility at every stop**: by the 0.4% rule the hall must
  read as "the market" from block-hero range on SILHOUETTE alone (the
  long roof + awning rhythm), not on signage. Signage carries the read
  only at player zoom, printed-card, model-scale.
- Fabrication: card hall + timber stall framing (the water-tower
  trestle's language), awning fabric as the one soft material.

**Canon**: requires a reference BEFORE geometry — candidate to fill the
open "stores reference" canon slot (owner intent, ARCHETYPE_DECISIONS).
**Function**: TBD pending economy notes (the player's market interface —
whatever it does, the building above is what it looks like doing it).
**Gate**: new registry entry; judged like stores at entry (declaration
precedes any geometry, exemptions printed with reasons).

## 2. REAL ESTATE OFFICE

**Soul** (drafted for the owner to shape — this one has no inherited
archetype):
- The QUIET counterpart to the marketplace: a modest storefront office
  with corner presence — where parcels are considered, not where crowds
  gather. Glazed frontage with LISTING CARDS displayed in the window:
  paper cards in a model shopfront, fabrication-honest, legible at
  player zoom as exactly what a real model-maker would print and glue.
- A lit interior (practicals) so it reads as OPEN — the placer's
  practicals field exists for exactly this anchor.
- Small massing, one storey + fascia; sited at a corner lot by
  preference (the placer can honour a corner-preference hint when
  siting rules arrive WITH the placement consumer, per the contract's
  named-futures discipline).

**Canon**: requires a reference BEFORE geometry — a storefront-office /
shopfront model reference, owner supplies or blesses.
**Function**: TBD pending economy notes.
**Gate — ruled by the design session, 2026-08-30: RIDES STORES' ENTRY.**
The test applied: an archetype entry exists so the gate knows what good
means for that type — so the question is not "is it a different kind of
building" but **"is there a criterion that would judge it
differently?"** On the drafted soul there is none: storefront, glazing,
trade-visible (listing cards are trade visible outside, made of paper),
honest signage, lit interior — all stores vocabulary already. An entry
with no distinct judging rule is a reserved name with no consumer — the
same cut applied to the placer schema that morning, applied to the
ruler's own lane. **The one thing that would change the answer, and it
is the OWNER'S look decision**: if the office must read as an OFFICE
AND NOT A SHOP — if a reader mistaking it for retail is a DEFECT rather
than a shrug — that is a distinct criterion, it earns its own entry,
and the entry carries that sentence as its reason.
**OWNER'S ANSWER (2026-08-30): RIDES STORES — mistaking it for a shop
is a shrug, not a defect.** The office's identity lives in context and
detail (the listing cards, the quiet corner), not in a gate rule. No
separate entry; settled.
